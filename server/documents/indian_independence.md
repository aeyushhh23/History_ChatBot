# The Indian Independence Movement (1857–1947)

The Indian Independence Movement was a series of historic events and campaigns aimed at ending British colonial rule in India. British involvement in the subcontinent began through the British East India Company, but after the Rebellion of 1857 (also known as the Sepoy Mutiny), the British Crown assumed direct control, initiating the period known as the British Raj. The movement for self-rule evolved from early regional rebellions into a massive nationwide struggle in the late 19th and 20th centuries.

The foundation for organized political resistance was laid in 1885 with the formation of the Indian National Congress (INC). By the early 20th century, calls for "Swaraj" (self-rule) grew stronger, championed by early nationalist leaders. The movement entered a new phase in 1915 with the return of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (Mahatma Gandhi) from South Africa. Gandhi introduced the concept of Satyagraha—passive, non-violent resistance based on truth—which galvanized millions of ordinary citizens.

Gandhi led several major nationwide campaigns, including the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922), which urged boycotts of British goods, courts, and schools, and the Civil Disobedience Movement, highlighted by the Salt March (Dandi March) in March 1930. During the Salt March, Gandhi walked 240 miles to the Arabian Sea to make salt in defiance of the British salt monopoly. Another critical milestone was the Quit India Movement in August 1942, demanding an immediate British withdrawal from India, which led to mass arrests of INC leaders, including Jawaharlal Nehru and Gandhi.

Alongside non-violent resistance, other approaches emerged. Subhas Chandra Bose led the Indian National Army (INA) during World War II, attempting to liberate India with external military alliances. Following the war, widespread civil unrest and economic strain convinced the British government that maintaining control was unsustainable. In July 1947, the British Parliament passed the Indian Independence Act, which partitioned the subcontinent into two sovereign nations: India and Pakistan. On August 15, 1947, India officially achieved independence, with Jawaharlal Nehru serving as its first Prime Minister.
