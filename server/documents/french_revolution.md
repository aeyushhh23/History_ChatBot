# The French Revolution (1789–1799)

The French Revolution was a watershed event in modern European history that began in 1789 and ended in 1799 with the ascent of Napoleon Bonaparte. During this period, French citizens radically altered their political landscape, uprooting centuries-old institutions such as the absolute monarchy and the feudal system. The upheaval was caused by widespread discontent with the French monarchy's financial crises, rigid social hierarchy (the Three Estates), and poor economic policies of King Louis XVI and his queen, Marie Antoinette.

The revolution formally commenced in May 1789, when the Estates-General was convened to resolve the financial crisis, but deadlock led the Third Estate (representing commoners) to declare itself the National Assembly. On June 20, 1789, they took the famous Tennis Court Oath, promising not to disband until France had a constitution. Tension peaked on July 14, 1789, when an armed mob stormed the Bastille, a medieval fortress and political prison in Paris, symbolizing the collapse of royal authority. Shortly after, the Assembly adopted the "Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen," asserting liberty, equality, and fraternity.

As the revolution progressed, political factions grew more radical. In September 1792, the monarchy was abolished, and the First French Republic was established. King Louis XVI was put on trial for treason and executed via guillotine in January 1793; Marie Antoinette met the same fate months later. The instability escalated into the Reign of Terror (1793–1794), led by Maximilien Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety. During this year-long purges, at least 17,000 political dissidents were formally executed.

Public opposition to the Reign of Terror eventually led to Robespierre's own arrest and execution in July 1794 (the Thermidorian Reaction). A moderate government known as the Directory took power in 1795 but struggled with financial inflation, corruption, and political instability. The revolution concluded on November 9, 1799 (18 Brumaire), when General Napoleon Bonaparte staged a coup d'état, establishing the French Consulate and eventually crowning himself Emperor.
